# Chapter 1. A Trial Is a Decision

This chapter asks what a clinical trial is for. It opens with SELECT, the semaglutide cardiovascular outcomes trial, and a ledger of what such a trial costs in patients, money and time, then argues that trialists should want a Bayesian approach because of the price of a trial, of being wrong and of waiting, and reviews evidence on how much efficiency real adaptive and Bayesian trials achieved and whether the approach is spreading. A counterweight section states the strongest objections. The chapter states the traditional trial fairly: a fixed sample size from a power calculation, randomization, alpha spending for interim looks, a multiplicity correction and a p-value in the clinical study report, together with what that apparatus costs. It then recasts the same choice as a decision, where a prior, a utility and a rule say when acting beats waiting, and shows that with a flat prior and one look the Bayesian rule is the one sided test. It previews the thesis and four mechanisms (the deviation scale, a single calibrated threshold with shrinkage, informative priors and optimal stopping), says what the regulator needs and does not need, gives a short history and maps the book. It proves nothing new; it fixes the vocabulary and the claim that the saving comes from monitoring, multiplicity and prior information and not from a lower bar.
