This chapter reconstructs landmark trials to show what a Bayesian design would have changed and what it would have cost. SELECT (semaglutide and cardiovascular events) is the main case: the trial as conducted, three monitoring schemes and SELECT's own design with one error rate, the saving in calendar time and exposure, what the monitoring committee would have seen, and what a committee and a regulator would have had to accept. REWIND (dulaglutide) is the case where the scheduled design wins, with a Bayesian reconstruction and the final data under skeptical priors. SURPASS CVOT is an active comparator with a noninferiority margin, TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 a prespecified biomarker strategy with baseline tau strata, and BNT162b2 a Bayesian confirmatory trial that led to an authorization. The chapter values the saving (events and quality adjusted life years, trial cost, timeline and the acceleration bound), compares the protocol side by side and states the limits of reconstruction.

## From the literature

SELECT (Lincoff and colleagues 2023, Ryan and colleagues 2020); REWIND (Gerstein and colleagues 2019); SURPASS CVOT (Nicholls and colleagues 2025) and the imputed MACE analysis of tirzepatide (Sattar and colleagues 2026); TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 (Sims and colleagues 2023); BNT162b2 (Polack and colleagues 2020, Pfizer statistical analysis plan, FDA emergency use review 2021 and the licensure 2021); FDA guidance on cardiovascular risk in diabetes drugs (2008); the cost of pivotal trials (Moore and colleagues 2018); the FDA single trial draft (2026) and the Bayesian draft (2026); the FDA adaptive design guidance (2019); ICH E9.

## New results

- [SELECT reconstruction, median events at decision with conditioning](../RESULTS.md#select_reconstruction_medians) (Section 21.1.2, page 476; computed): In our reconstruction of SELECT at hazard ratio 0.80, a calibrated Bayesian rule stops at a median of 464 events among trials that declare efficacy and 486 over all trials, against 817 for SELECT's planned design (one interim analysis with Lan-DeMets spending, then the final); the e value rule and a four look design are slower or less powerful. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026), Bayesian versus Standard Clinical Trial Design: Theory, Evidence, and Protocol, working paper.
