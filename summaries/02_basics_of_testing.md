This chapter, Basics of Testing, builds the comparison of two treatments from the ground up with simulated and real trial data. It starts with one trial and two endpoints, reruns it many times, and derives the randomization test, the normal approximation and the z test. It defines the p value carefully, explains confidence intervals and their duality with tests, and defines type I error, type II error and power. It explains why a one sided level of 0.025 is used, and covers noninferiority and equivalence against a margin, with the SURPASS CVOT noninferiority margin as an example. It then assembles the traditional trial: sample size from alpha and power, equal allocation, group sequential spending, multiplicity control, the clinical study report, and what the apparatus leaves out. A section on dichotomizing outcomes shows what cutting a continuous measurement into responders costs in information, power and sample size. A section on ordinal outcomes compares dichotomizing, shift analysis and utility weights, using the modified Rankin scale and the utility weighted version that was the primary endpoint of DAWN. Other examples are RECOVERY (a rate ratio and an interval) and ANDROMEDA SHOCK (what not significant does and does not mean).

## From the literature

Randomization and the logic of the test (Fisher 1935, 1946); the Neyman and Pearson framework for errors and power (1933); the design of experiments (Cox 1958); p value misreadings (Goodman 2008) and the ASA statement (Wasserstein and Lazar 2016); interval methods for proportions (Newcombe 1998); noninferiority testing (Blackwelder 1982, Dunnett and Gent 1977, Rothmann and colleagues 2003); FDA noninferiority guidance (2016); group sequential spending and boundaries (Lan and DeMets 1983, O'Brien and Fleming 1979); the cost of dichotomizing (Senn 2003, Altman and Royston 2006, Snapinn and Jiang 2007, Fedorov and colleagues 2009); utility weights for the modified Rankin scale (Chaisinanunkul and colleagues 2015); DAWN (Nogueira and colleagues 2018); SURPASS CVOT (Nicholls and colleagues 2025); REWIND (Gerstein and colleagues 2019); RECOVERY (2021); ANDROMEDA SHOCK (Hernandez and colleagues 2019); ICH E9 and E10.
