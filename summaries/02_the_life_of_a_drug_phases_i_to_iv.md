This chapter walks through the life of a drug, Phase I to IV, and asks at each phase which decision is made and with what evidence. Phase I looks for safety and a dose, Phase II for signs of activity and the dose to carry forward, Phase III for confirmation and the regulatory decision, and Phase IV for what appears after approval. The chapter treats the phases as a sequence of options, where each phase buys information that is worth having only if it can change the next decision. It covers bioequivalence by two one sided tests (TOST) and a posterior summary, and then reads the phases in Bayesian terms: what a prior from one phase becomes in the next, and where the traditional separation of phases hides information. The rofecoxib story (VIGOR, APPROVe) appears as a Phase IV lesson on safety that a trial too small for harm cannot see. It proves nothing new.
