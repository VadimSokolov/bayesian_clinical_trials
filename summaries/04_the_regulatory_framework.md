This chapter explains the legal and regulatory frame in which a trial is designed, and which documents govern which method. It begins with the statutory standard of substantial evidence in section 505(d) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, the 1997 amendment that allows one trial with confirmatory evidence, the regulation 21 CFR 314.126 on what adequate and well controlled means, and the 2026 draft on substantial evidence. A table then maps each method of the traditional trial (control group, randomization, blinding, sample size, interim analyses, multiplicity, noninferiority, the estimand, covariate adjustment) to the regulation or guidance that asks for it and how strongly. The chapter describes a traditional submission and what changes with a Bayesian adaptive design, goes through the ICH principles and the FDA design guidances, the history of Bayesian methods at the regulators and the January 2026 FDA draft on Bayesian methodology with the response to it. It separates what documents require, recommend and permit, describes how to interact with FDA (formal meetings, the Complex Innovative Trial Design and MIDD paired meeting programs, CPIM, drug development tool qualification), and gives the EMA and MHRA counterparts. Case studies include REBYOTA, BNT162b2, pediatric belimumab, and the devices Superion and OPTIMIZER Smart.

## From the literature

The standard of substantial evidence (FD&C Act 505(d), FDAMA 1997, 21 CFR 314.126); FDA guidance on adaptive designs (2019), master protocols (2022, revised 2026), multiple endpoints (2022), noninferiority (2016), covariate adjustment (2023), external controls (2023), expedited programs (2014), formal meetings (2026), the Bayesian draft (2026) and the single trial draft (2026); the 2010 FDA guidance on Bayesian statistics in medical device trials (Campbell and colleagues 2011, 2023, Pennello and Thompson 2008); the Complex Innovative Trial Design program (FDA 2020); the 21st Century Cures Act (2016); ICH E9, E9(R1), E10 (2000), E8(R1), E11A and E20 and M15; EMA reflection papers on adaptive designs and platform trials and the Bayesian concept paper; Ruberg and colleagues (2023) on Bayesian approaches in drug development; Evans and colleagues, Gelman and colleagues, and Lee and colleagues (all 2026) on the FDA draft.
