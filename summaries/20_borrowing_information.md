# Chapter 20. Borrowing Information

This chapter asks how external information can enter a trial without bias. It starts from what the traditional design does with prior data and the traditional objections to external and historical controls, then gives the Bayesian idea: exchangeability, with the hierarchical model developed in the separate chapter on hierarchical pooling, power priors and the normalized power prior (with a rare disease example that chooses the discount by marginal likelihood), commensurate priors, and meta analytic predictive priors with robust mixtures. It measures borrowing by effective sample size and conflict, and the cost of borrowing in type I error and power under drift, and what the regulator does with this. It compares traditional and Bayesian designs side by side and covers pediatric extrapolation and ICH E11A, noninferiority with borrowed information and margin synthesis, indirect comparison through a common comparator (the tirzepatide chain), borrowing across subgroups in basket trials, and a case where borrowing failed (an NSCLC external control). It also covers prognostic covariate adjustment and digital twins, and gives a decision guide. Cases: REBYOTA (told in full in the chapter of landmark trials), pediatric belimumab and OPTIMIZER Smart.

## From the literature

Historical controls (Pocock 1976); the power prior (Ibrahim and Chen 2000, Neuenschwander, Branson and Spiegelhalter 2009); commensurate priors (Hobbs and colleagues 2011, 2012); meta analytic predictive priors (Neuenschwander and colleagues 2010, Schmidli and colleagues 2014, Weber and colleagues 2021); exchangeability and nonexchangeability (Neuenschwander and colleagues 2016, Hobbs and colleagues 2018, Viele and colleagues 2014, Kaizer and colleagues 2018, Galwey 2017); propensity score integrated priors (Wang and colleagues 2019, Lin and colleagues 2019); basket trials (Thall and colleagues 2003, Berry and colleagues 2013, Simon and colleagues 2016, Chu and Yuan 2018); noninferiority margins (Fleming 2008, Rothmann and colleagues 2003, Snapinn and Jiang 2008); SURPASS CVOT (Nicholls and colleagues 2025) and REWIND (Gerstein and colleagues 2019); pediatric extrapolation (Dunne and colleagues 2011, Gamalo Siebers and colleagues 2017, ICH E11A); FDA and EMA guidance on external controls, rare diseases and real world evidence (2023, 2025), MHRA 2025; the EMA qualification of PROCOVA (2022); the REBYOTA review (FDA 2022); belimumab (Brunner and colleagues 2020); OPTIMIZER Smart (FDA 2019).
