This chapter treats safety, where the question is whether harm is present and what to do about it. It starts with the rofecoxib cumulative signal: what the traditional analysis said, what a Bayesian reading adds, a move from inference to decision, and the naproxen hypothesis as a model comparison. It treats continuous monitoring with a posterior probability and an e-value, signals in spontaneous reports (the traditional proportional reporting measure and the gamma Poisson shrinker), toxicity monitoring inside a trial (the traditional counterpart, the Bayesian rule and operating characteristics), and aggregate safety assessment, which joins blinded pooled review, unblinded committee review, hierarchical models across adverse event terms and surveillance by a maximized sequential probability ratio test. It connects the two views and says what the regulator will ask.

## From the literature

The VIGOR and APPROVe trials (Bombardier and colleagues 2000, Bresalier and colleagues 2005) and the rofecoxib record (Juni and colleagues 2004, Curfman and colleagues 2005, Rothman 2004); the gamma Poisson shrinker (DuMouchel 1999); Bayesian signal detection (Bate and colleagues 1998, Szarfman and colleagues 2002, van Puijenbroek and colleagues 2002, Evans and colleagues 2001, Madigan and colleagues 2011, Zorych and colleagues 2013); sequential toxicity monitoring (Thall, Simon and Estey 1995, 1996, Ivanova and colleagues 2005); the maximized sequential probability ratio test (Kulldorff and colleagues 2011); Bayesian monitoring of blinded data (Gould and Wang 2017); hierarchical models for adverse events (Berry and Berry 2004, Xia, Ma and Carlin 2011, Mehrotra and Adewale 2012); Ville (1939) for the e-value monitor; FDA documents on data monitoring committees (2006, 2024), IND safety reporting (2025), FAERS and 21 CFR 312.32; ICH E2A; the EMA good pharmacovigilance practices addendum.
