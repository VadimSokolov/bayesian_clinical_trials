# Chapter 19. Borrowing Information

This chapter asks how external information can enter a trial without bias. It starts from what the traditional design does with prior data and the traditional objections to external and historical controls, then gives the Bayesian idea: exchangeability and hierarchical models, power priors and the normalized power prior (with a rare disease example that chooses the discount by marginal likelihood), commensurate priors, and meta analytic predictive priors with robust mixtures. It measures borrowing by effective sample size and conflict, and the cost of borrowing in type I error and power under drift. It covers pediatric extrapolation and ICH E11A, noninferiority with borrowed information and margin synthesis, indirect comparison through a common comparator (the tirzepatide chain), borrowing across subgroups in basket trials, and a case where borrowing failed (an NSCLC external control). It also covers prognostic covariate adjustment and digital twins, and gives a decision guide. Cases: REBYOTA, pediatric belimumab and OPTIMIZER Smart.
