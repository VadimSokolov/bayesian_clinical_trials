This chapter asks how Bayesian methods have actually been used in well known trials, and what each design, decision rule and regulatory response teaches. For every trial it gives the question, the design, the prespecified decision rule and its thresholds, what happened, what regulators accepted, and a lesson. The cases are I-SPY 2 (graduation by Bayesian predictive probability, response adaptive randomization in breast cancer), REMAP-CAP (a multifactorial adaptive platform trial with domains, an ordinal endpoint and the interleukin 6 receptor antagonist result), the heparin multiplatform trial of REMAP-CAP, ATTACC and ACTIV-4a (one hierarchical model with dynamic borrowing across severity cohorts), BNT162b2 (a Bayesian success rule on vaccine efficacy with a Beta prior), REBYOTA (a hierarchical model with dynamic borrowing so one phase 3 trial stands in for two), GBM AGILE (a seamless phase 2/3 registration platform), pediatric extrapolation for empagliflozin and linagliptin (the DINAMO trial with borrowing from adults), DAWN (adaptive enrichment with a utility weighted modified Rankin scale), and SNAP (a platform trial where the noninferiority margin decides the claim). It shows that thresholds are calibrated by simulation and fixed in advance.

## From the literature

I-SPY 2 is described by Barker and colleagues (2009), Park and colleagues (2016), Nanda and colleagues (2020) and Berry (2015, 2025). REMAP-CAP is presented by Angus and colleagues (2020) with the interleukin 6 result of the REMAP-CAP Investigators (Gordon and colleagues 2021); Angus (2015) is cited for embedding trials in care. The heparin reports are Goligher and colleagues (2021) and Lawler and colleagues (2021). BNT162b2 is reported by Polack and colleagues (2020), with the Pfizer statistical analysis plan and the FDA emergency use and Comirnaty documents (2021). REBYOTA rests on the FDA review documents (2022). GBM AGILE is described by Alexander and colleagues (2018) and Wen and colleagues (2026), with temporal drift by Saville and colleagues (2022). DINAMO is Laffel and colleagues (2023) with Sailer and colleagues (2025) and ICH E11A (2024). DAWN is Jovin and colleagues (2017) and Nogueira and colleagues (2018). SNAP is Lee and colleagues (2026). The FDA Bayesian draft guidance (2026) names several of these cases.
