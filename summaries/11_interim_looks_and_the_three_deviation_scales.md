This chapter asks how to look at accumulating data without losing control of the error. It shows why repeated testing at a fixed level fails, develops the Armitage recursion, and compares O'Brien-Fleming, Pocock and the families between them, plus other boundary families (Armitage plans, the triangular test and designs with futility boundaries). It covers spending functions and the information fraction, futility boundaries (binding and non binding), and what a data monitoring committee does. The Bayesian sequential problem follows: the posterior does not depend on the stopping rule, a short history of Bayesian monitoring, the Bayes sequential decision problem, and Wald's test with its large deviation ledger. The chapter then develops the three deviation scales and their tolls, where the surcharge comes from, the seventy percent result, four boundaries compared and a sequential power calculation. It compares traditional and Bayesian monitoring side by side, runs one outcomes trial under three rulebooks, and gives a reporting template for the monitoring committee. Cases: truncated trials overestimate (Bassler and colleagues), heparin in Covid 19, the EOLIA futility stop and the Women's Health Initiative stop for harm.

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [The three deviation scales (evidence tolls)](../RESULTS.md#evidence_tolls_three_scales) (Section 11.9.2, page 253; proved): A fixed level test, a Bayes rule and an anytime valid rule act at three different sizes of the squared standardized score; for the normal mixture the anytime valid bar exceeds the Bayes bar by a surcharge that is about 2 log(1/alpha) at large information. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026), Bayesian versus Standard Clinical Trial Design: Theory, Evidence, and Protocol, working paper.
- [The surcharge for validity at every stopping time is about 70 per cent of the fixed trial requirement, as a comparison of boundary terms](../RESULTS.md#surcharge_seventy_percent) (Section 11.9.4, page 256; proved): For the normal mixture e value rule, against the uncalibrated Bayes factor bar (posterior odds of one), the large information surcharge 7.38 is about 70 per cent of the fixed trial standardized requirement 10.51; this compares boundary terms and is not an exact sample size identity. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026), Bayesian versus Standard Clinical Trial Design: Theory, Evidence, and Protocol, working paper.
