This chapter treats time to event endpoints. It starts with the traditional analysis (Kaplan and Meier, the log rank test and the Cox model), then shows that events are the information, derives the gamma posterior for a hazard, and gives predictive probability in an event driven trial. A section on non proportional hazards covers why the hazard ratio misdescribes a delayed or cure type effect, power loss under a delayed effect, and tests built for such effects (weighted log rank, restricted mean survival time and modestly weighted tests). It ends with regulation and practice, key points and exercises.

## From the literature

The product limit estimator (Kaplan and Meier 1958); the log rank test (Mantel 1966, Peto and Peto 1972); the proportional hazards model and partial likelihood (Cox 1972, 1975, Breslow 1974, Kalbfleisch and Prentice 1978); event driven sample size (Schoenfeld 1981, 1983, Freedman 1982); early life table and survival models (Berkson and Gage 1952, Boag 1949, Irwin 1949, Holford 1976); weighted log rank and the Fleming and Harrington class (Harrington and Fleming 1982, Fleming and Harrington 1991); modestly weighted tests (Magirr and Burman 2019); restricted mean survival time (Karrison 1987, Royston and Parmar 2011, 2013, Uno and colleagues 2014, Trinquart and colleagues 2016); the hazard ratio as an estimand (Hernan 2010, Struthers and Kalbfleisch 1986, Alexander and colleagues 2018); Bayesian survival models (Ibrahim, Chen and Sinha 2001, Chen, Ibrahim and Sinha 1999, Murray and colleagues 2014, Roychoudhury and colleagues 2021); FDA guidance on oncology endpoints (2018), adaptive designs (2019), ICH E9 and E9(R1), and the Bayesian draft (2026).
